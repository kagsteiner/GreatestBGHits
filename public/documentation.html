What is Second Roll?

Second Roll is a learning tool for DailyGammon players who want to improve their backgammon skills. The app analyzes your past games on DailyGammon with the local Hedgehog backgammon engine, finds checker-play and cube-decision mistakes, and creates personalized quizzes to help you learn from them. Standard backgammon and Nackgammon matches are both supported.

Think of it as a personal backgammon coach that shows you the exact positions where you went wrong and asks you: “What would you do now?”

Second Roll has been designed with PC / Mac screens in mind. It works nicely on iPads/Safari. With other devices / browsers, you might need to scale the UI to fit your screen.

Getting Started

When you first open Second Roll, you’ll see a login screen asking for your DailyGammon credentials.

First-Time Setup

  1. A modal window (pop-up box) will appear automatically
  2. Enter your DailyGammon username
  3. Enter your DailyGammon password
  4. Click Save and continue

Important: Your login information is stored securely and is only used to fetch your games from DailyGammon.

The Home Screen

After logging in, you’ll see three main buttons:

  1. Add New Quizzes - Fetches your recent DailyGammon games and creates quiz questions
  2. Take the Quiz - Practice with quiz questions from your past mistakes
  3. Statistics - See how well you’re doing and which positions give you the most trouble

Need to change your login? Click “Update DailyGammon login” at the bottom of the home screen anytime.

Adding New Quizzes

When you’re ready to create quiz questions from your recent games:

  1. Click “Add New Quizzes” on the home screen
  2. Choose how many days of games to analyze (default: 30 days)
  3. Click the “Start” button

What Happens Next?

The app will:

  • Download your recent completed matches from DailyGammon
  • Replay every game in the correct order, including cube ownership, score, Crawford state, and the Nackgammon starting position where applicable
  • Use Hedgehog to evaluate both checker plays and legal cube decisions
  • Find significant mistakes made by you or your opponents
  • Create checker-play, double/no-double, and take/pass quizzes from those mistakes

Checker plays are analyzed cubefully: the current cube value, cube owner, match score, and match length are part of Hedgehog’s evaluation. Cube decisions use match-aware equity as well, so the correct answer can change dramatically with the score.

This process can take time - analyzing matches thoroughly takes several minutes per game. The more days you select, the longer it will take. If you wish, go back by using the “Back to home” link in the top area, and select statistics to see how many quizzes have been added. As soon as a quiz has been added you can start quizzing, while more quizzes will be added in the background.

Understanding the Progress Screen

While the analysis runs, you’ll see:

  • Matches: How many matches have been analyzed (for example, “5/12” means 5 out of 12 are complete)
  • Quizzes added: New quiz questions created during this run
  • Total quizzes: Your complete quiz library
  • Progress bar: A visual indication of completion
  • Log window: Reports each new item as a cube quiz or checker play quiz, with a running count for both types

Important Notes

  • Be patient: The first time you run this, it may take 30-60 minutes or more, depending on how many games you’re analyzing
  • Feel free to go away: The process will automatically finish without you. If you navigate away, it will continue in the background
  • Queue system: If multiple users are analyzing matches, you may need to wait your turn. The screen will show how many users are ahead of you
  • Nackgammon games: Nackgammon matches are supported. Second Roll recognizes the variant, reconstructs its different starting checker placement, and analyzes checker and cube decisions normally. Their quizzes are labeled Nack Match so you can distinguish them from regular backgammon positions.

Taking Quizzes

This is the heart of the app. Click “Take the Quiz” from the home screen.

Choosing a Training Mode

Checker play and cube handling are different skills, so the selector at the top of the quiz page lets you train them separately or together:

  • Checker + Cube: Mixes both kinds of decisions and alternates between them when both are available
  • Checker play: Shows only positions where you must choose how to play a dice roll
  • Cube decisions: Shows only double/no-double and take/pass decisions

Your choice is remembered in the browser. If no active quiz matches the selected mode or filters, the page tells you instead of showing an unrelated question.

Checker-Play Quizzes

A checker-play quiz shows the dice and offers up to four moves: Hedgehog’s best move, the move played in the match, and useful alternatives where available. Hedgehog evaluates the moves cubefully rather than pretending that the doubling cube does not exist.

Cube-Decision Quizzes

Cube quizzes always have two answers because the real decision is binary:

  • Double or no double when the player on roll owns the right to offer the cube
  • Take or pass when a double has been offered

Second Roll checks for missed doubles before eligible rolls, evaluates doubles that were actually offered, and grades the opponent’s response. It respects cube ownership and the current cube value. There is no cube question for the opening roll, during a Crawford game, or whenever a player is not legally allowed to double.

Visual documentation

Click on the thumbnails to see the full size images. The images describe all the UI elements of the quiz interface.

The Quiz Interface

When you open a quiz, you’ll see:

  1. The backgammon board - showing the position from one of your games
  2. Match information - If this was a match (not money game), you’ll see the score and match length
  3. The dice roll - displayed for checker-play quizzes
  4. The decision prompt - the correct checker color is named as on roll or responding to a cube
  5. Multiple choice options - 2-4 checker plays, or the two legal cube actions

Understanding the Board

The board displays: - Blue checkers (Player 1) - moving clockwise - Red checkers (Player 2) - moving counterclockwise - Point numbers - Shown from the perspective of whichever player is on roll. - The bar - The vertical divider in the middle (where hit checkers go) - Bear-off area - Right side, showing how many checkers each player has borne off - Pip count - Total distance remaining for each player (lower is better) - The cube - Left side, showing the doubling cube value and who owns it

Board orientation tips: If the board orientation feels backwards to you, click the “⇵ Board” button at the top to flip it vertically (swap top and bottom rows). For a left-right mirror—swapping which side the points appear on—click the "⇄" button in the center strip of the board (between the dice and the two rows of points). Both settings are remembered for your next session.

Point Numbering

The numbers on the board change based on whose turn it is. This matches how backgammon players think: - Your 1-point is always your home board point closest to bearing off - Your 24-point is always the furthest point from bearing off - When Blue is on roll, you see Blue’s point numbers - When Red is on roll, you see Red’s point numbers

How to Answer

  1. Read the position carefully - Check the match score, pip count, and cube position
  2. Identify the decision - For checker play, inspect the dice; for a cube quiz, note whether you are deciding to double or responding with take/pass
  3. Review the options - Compare the available checker plays or the two cube actions
  4. Select your answer - Click the radio button next to the move you think is best
  5. Click “Rate my guess” - The app will tell you if you’re correct

Understanding the Feedback

After you submit your answer, you’ll see:

  • “Correct!”, “Pretty good!”, or “Not quite.” - Whether you selected the best move, a move within 0.020 equity of it, or a worse move. Both the best and near-best selections count as a solved quiz.
  • All options with their equity values:
    • Best - The computer’s best move (shown with actual equity like “+0.543”)
    • played - The move or cube action actually chosen in your game (shown with equity difference like “(-0.234)”) - the number you see tells you how many points of equity your selection is worse than the best move.
    • Alt - Other reasonable alternatives (also shown with equity difference)

About equity: Positive values favor the player making the decision; negative values favor the opponent. The difference between two values shows the cost of choosing the weaker action. For match cube decisions, Second Roll uses Hedgehog’s normalized match equity so that a mistake is measured meaningfully even when raw match-winning percentages are close together.

View on DailyGammon

After seeing the answer, a “View on DailyGammon” button appears. Click it to see: - The complete game this position came from - All the moves before and after this position - Your opponent’s name and the final result

This is helpful for understanding the context of the position.

Analyze on Hedgehog

After seeing the answer, select “Analyze on Hedgehog” to open the position in Hedgehog’s interactive Study screen. Scroll to the bottom of Hedgehog’s Edit position panel and select Apply to view the full analysis. You can then experiment with alternative moves, dice, cube decisions, models, and analysis settings.

Moving to the Next Question

Click “Next” to get another quiz question. The app uses a smart system: - Questions you answer correctly appear less frequently - Questions you miss appear more often - This helps you focus on positions that challenge you most

Filtering by Opponent

At the top of the quiz page, you’ll see a dropdown menu that holds your username.

Why Filter by Opponent?

Usually you will select your name there, to learn from your own mistakes.

But if you’re curious, or want to look at the mistakes of a specific opponent (maybe a very strong one) then select the respective name.

How to Use the Filter

  1. Click the dropdown with your username
  2. Select an opponent’s name, or your own name
  3. The quiz will only show positions from that player
  4. Select “All players” again to see positions from all your games

Note: The opponent list shows everyone you’ve played against in your analyzed games.

Filtering by Match

Next to the opponent dropdown, you'll see a second dropdown that lists your matches.

Why Filter by Match?

If you use second roll for a longer time, you will find the number of quizzes to be a bit overwhelming. You have hundrds of quizzes and won't come across the same quiz twice. Filter by match and you'll be able to re-visit wrong answers more often.

How to Use the Match Filter

  1. Click the dropdown that says "All matches"
  2. Select a match (each entry shows the match ID, length, e.g. 5pt or 11pt, and opponent)
  3. The quiz will only show positions from that match
  4. Select "All matches" again to see positions from all your games

Note: You can combine the opponent filter and the match filter—for example, pick an opponent first, then narrow down to a specific match.

Statistics Page

Click “Statistics” from the home screen to see your progress.

What You’ll See

  1. Overall Correctness Rate - Big percentage showing how often you choose the best move
  2. Total Quizzes Taken - How many times you’ve answered questions
  3. Correct Answers - How many questions you’ve answered correctly
  4. Available Quizzes - Total quiz questions in your library
  5. Checker/Cube breakdown - Separate availability, attempts, and correctness figures for checker play and cube decisions

Worst Performing Quizzes

Below your statistics, you’ll see a list of quiz positions that give you the most trouble. Each entry shows: - The best move for that position - Your success rate on that specific position - How many times you’ve attempted it

Why This Is Helpful: These are your weak spots! Click on any position to practice it again immediately.

Understanding Board Colors

Throughout the app, colors have consistent meanings:

  • Blue / Black checkers = Player 1 in the dark / classic theme
  • Red / White checkers = Player 2 in the dark / classic theme
  • Checker and cube prompts use these colors instead of generic player numbers, so it is always clear who is making the decision

The board automatically adjusts so the player on roll is always shown from their perspective.

Tips for Best Results

1. Start Small

Don’t analyze 60 days of games on your first session. Start with 7-14 days to get a feel for the app.

2. Regular Practice

Taking a few quizzes daily is more effective than cramming. Make it part of your backgammon routine.

3. Focus on Patterns

When you miss a question, try to understand why the best move is better. Look for patterns: - Is it about timing? - Is it about flexibility? - Is it about race calculations? - Is it about avoiding getting hit?

4. Use the DailyGammon Link

After seeing the answer, check the full game on DailyGammon. Understanding what happened before and after the position gives valuable context.

5. Check Your Statistics

Review your statistics page regularly to see which types of positions trouble you most.

6. Re-analyze Periodically

As you play more games on DailyGammon, run “Add New Quizzes” again to get fresh material. Your game evolves, so your mistakes will too!

Common Questions

“Why is the board upside down?”

The board uses the same orientation that DailyGammon shows when you're viewing the match. If you prefer it the other way, click the “⇵ Board” button in the header to flip it vertically, and the "🔓 Lock" button to lock it in that orientation. Additionally use the "⇄" button in the center strip to mirror it horizontally.

“Why do some positions have only 2 choices?”

Every cube quiz has exactly two choices: double/no double or take/pass. Checker-play quizzes include Hedgehog’s best move, the move actually played, and up to two alternatives. If some checker moves are equivalent, you will see fewer distinct choices.

“What if I disagree with the ‘best’ move?”

The analysis is performed locally by Hedgehog using a strong neural-network model. No computer is perfect, but the equity differences are reliable enough to identify significant mistakes. If you disagree, use the Analyze on Hedgehog button to explore the position and compare alternatives, or use another strong backgammon program for a second opinion.

If you want to compare the complete match in BGBlitz:

  1. Click the “View on Dailygammon” button.
  2. In DailyGammon, click on “List of moves”
  3. Copy the URL to the clipboard (click on the URL in your browser, then CTRL-C / Command-C)
  4. Start BGBlitz
  5. Immediately type CTRL-V / Command-V. BGBlitz will analyze the match
  6. In DailyGammon look for the match standing, the roll etc to find the move. It should be marked as red in BGBlitz’s move list because it is a blunder.
  7. Have a look at equity, win ratio, gammon/bg ratio etc to understand why the computer prefers this move.

“Why did analysis take so long?”

Quality backgammon analysis requires downloading each game’s complete move list, reconstructing every position, checking every eligible cube decision before the roll, comparing each checker play with its legal alternatives, and selecting useful quiz answers. Hedgehog performs all of those evaluations locally on the Second Roll server.

This thoroughness ensures you get meaningful learning material, but it takes time.

“The ‘Take the Quiz’ button is disabled. Why?”

This means you don’t have any quiz questions yet. Click “Add New Quizzes” first to create questions from your games.

“Why do I see no double decisions?”

Select Cube decisions or Checker + Cube on the quiz page. Cube quizzes are created only for significant mistakes; the opening roll, Crawford games, and positions where doubling is not legal do not create cube questions.

“Are Nackgammon matches supported?”

Yes. Second Roll reconstructs the Nackgammon starting layout and supports both checker-play and cube-decision analysis. These quizzes are identified by a Nack Match label above the board.

“I want to bear off to the left side, where can I change this”

Sorry, also not supported yet.

“Why can’t I make my move directly on the board”

Because this is a multiple choice quiz :-) (I miss this feature as well, maybe someday…)

How secure are my data?”

I am reusing the login mechanism of DailyGammon. This means that, given that DailyGammon's security is weak due to the use of HTTP, your data is as secure as your DailyGammon account.

“Why am I browsing to secondroll.de but the browser shows a different, complicated URL?”

This is normal and happens for technical reasons. The app redirects you to a special URL during authentication and quiz sessions to make sure your experience is secure and personalized. You don’t need to worry—this is expected behavior and does not affect your privacy or security.

“What is the quality of the move analysis, and can I change it?”

Second Roll uses the local Hedgehog community engine for both checker-play and cube analysis. The neural-network model and search depths are configured by the server operator. The application default is the FOX v0.3 model with 2-ply checker and cube analysis. Individual users cannot change these settings, which keeps quiz evaluations consistent for everyone.

"What does the debug toggle do?"

When you report a problem, I may ask you for the quiz ID. Turn on the debug toggle and a text field will appear showing the current quiz's ID. Copy and paste it into your email so I can reproduce the issue.

Glossary of Backgammon Terms

Here are terms used throughout the app:

  • Blunder: A significant mistake that costs equity; Second Roll applies separate significance thresholds to checker play and cube decisions
  • Equity: The average number of points you expect to win/lose from a position
  • Bear off / Bearing off: Removing checkers from the board once they’re all in your home board
  • The bar: The raised center divider where hit checkers are placed
  • Pip count: Total number of pips needed to bear off all checkers
  • On roll: The player whose turn it is
  • Home board: Your points 1-6
  • Outer board: Your points 7-12
  • Match play: Games played to a certain number of points (e.g., “Match to 5”)
  • Cube / Doubling cube: The die showing 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, or 64 used for doubling stakes
  • Cube owner: The player who last accepted a double (or “centered” if neither owns it)
  • Double / No double: The decision whether to offer the doubling cube before rolling
  • Take / Pass: The opponent’s response to a double; taking continues at twice the cube value, while passing concedes the current cube value
  • Crawford game: The first game after either player reaches one point from winning the match; the doubling cube may not be used
  • Nackgammon: A backgammon variant with a different starting checker layout
  • Hedgehog: The local backgammon engine Second Roll uses to evaluate checker plays and cube decisions

Privacy & Data

What Data Is Stored?

  • Your DailyGammon username (but not your password permanently)
  • Your quiz positions from analyzed games
  • Your quiz performance statistics (correct/incorrect answers)

What Data Is NOT Stored?

  • Complete game records (only the mistake positions are kept)
  • Any personal information beyond your username
  • Your full game history

Who Can See My Data?

Only you can see your quiz questions and statistics. Each user has their own private database of positions.

I am developing this app for fun, because I want to use it, and to give something back to the DailyGammon community. I am not interested in making money, and never will. I will not sell your data. The app will not show ads.

If you’re a total privacy nut and want to know for sure: have a look at the source code: github.com/kagsteiner/GreatestBGHits.

Vibe Coded with Codex

Second Roll is a vibe-coded application. I develop the whole app in conversation with OpenAI Codex: I bring the backgammon knowledge, product ideas, production experience, and final decisions; Codex helps turn those conversations into working software.

This collaboration covers the complete project: the browser interface, server, database tools, DailyGammon integration, Hedgehog analysis, Nackgammon handling, cube-decision logic, automated tests, debugging, documentation, and deployment procedures. Features are discussed first, implemented incrementally, tested, and then tried against real matches. The cube trainer itself grew exactly this way—from a conversation about whether checker play and cube handling should be separate skills into a production feature.

“Vibe coded” does not mean nobody checks the result. I review the behavior, test it with my own matches, compare difficult positions with established backgammon software, and decide what reaches the production server. Codex helps investigate failures and write tests so that fixes remain fixed. The source code is public, so you can inspect both the result and the project’s continuing evolution.

Have Fun Learning!

Remember: Every mistake is a learning opportunity. The positions in these quizzes are from your real games, which means they represent the exact situations where you can improve most.

Don’t get discouraged if your correctness rate starts low - that’s normal! As you practice, you’ll see patterns emerging, and your decision-making will improve.

Good luck, and may your blunders become victories!
